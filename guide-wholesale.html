Buying Wholesale vs Retail

How to reduce your cost per melt by understanding wholesale pricing and UK supplier tiers.

Home / Buying Guides / Pricing & Sourcing

Buying your supplies at the right price is essential to building a profitable wax melt business. This guide explains when to buy retail, when to go wholesale, and how to get the best value from UK suppliers.

Most UK suppliers charge less per kg as the bag gets bigger. Here are real examples, checked on the suppliers' own websites on 28 October 2026. Prices are as shown on each site and change often, so check before you order.

When to Buy Retail

Buying retail (small quantities, standard prices) makes sense when:

  • You're a hobbyist making occasional batches
  • You're testing a new wax, fragrance or supplier for the first time
  • You need a specific item quickly without minimum order requirements
  • Storage space is limited

When to Go Wholesale

Moving to wholesale purchasing is the right decision when:

  • You're making consistent batches (at least weekly)
  • You have storage space for 5–25kg bags of wax and bulk fragrance
  • You've settled on a recipe and no longer need to test extensively
  • You're selling products and need to maximise your profit margins

  • Craftovator — excellent bulk pricing on wax, fragrance and dyes
  • Livemoor — soy wax from 100g up to 20kg, with a wholesale and trade orders page
  • The Soap Kitchen — great for makers who also produce soap and cosmetics

Calculating Your Cost Per Melt

Before deciding on your wholesale volume, calculate your true cost per melt. Include:

  • Wax cost per gram (weight per melt × cost per kg ÷ 1000)
  • Fragrance oil cost per gram (at your chosen % load)
  • Colourant cost per melt
  • Packaging cost per unit
  • Labels, delivery, time

Most makers target a material cost of 20–30% of the retail selling price, leaving margin for time, overheads and profit.

Buying Fragrance Oils in Bulk

Fragrance oils are often the most expensive ingredient by weight, and most suppliers charge less per ml as the bottle gets bigger. Prices vary a lot between suppliers and scents, so compare the per-ml price on each supplier's site before you order. Always test a scent at small scale before committing to a litre of it.
