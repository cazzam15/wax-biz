Do I Need Insurance?

What the law actually requires when you sell wax melts in the UK, which cover makers usually choose, and what to ask before you buy a policy.

Home / Buying Guides / Insurance

"Do I need insurance to sell wax melts?" comes up again and again with new UK makers. The short answer: if you work on your own, the law doesn't make you buy business insurance. But you are legally responsible for the products you make, and many craft fairs and shops won't take you without cover. This guide explains the difference, in plain English.

What the law actually requires

The government's Business.gov.uk insurance guide lists only two kinds of business insurance that are a legal must:

  • Employers' liability insurance, if people work for you.
  • Motor insurance, if you use a vehicle for the business, including your own car.

So a sole maker selling online or at markets doesn't have to buy public or product liability by law. The insurer AXA says the same for candle and wax melt businesses: "Unless you have employees, business insurance isn't a legal requirement to sell candles in the UK."

Why most makers get cover anyway

Even without a policy, you are still liable for what you sell. Under section 2 of the Consumer Protection Act 1987, the producer of a product is liable for damage caused by a defect in it. So is anyone who puts their name or brand on it. That means you, the maker whose name is on the label.

Wax melts are used with heat, and fragrance oils can cause skin reactions, which is why your CLP label carries warnings. If a customer says your melt caused an injury or damaged their home, you would have to deal with the claim and any legal costs yourself. Insurance is there to pay for that.

The main types of cover

CoverWhat it protects againstDo wax melt makers need it?
Products liabilityClaims that something you made or sold caused illness, injury or damageStrongly recommended as soon as you sell
Public liabilityClaims that a member of the public was hurt, or their property damaged, by your business activitiesRecommended, and often required for craft fairs and markets
Employers' liabilityClaims from employees injured or made ill by their workA legal must once you employ someone (with a few exceptions, such as close family)
Stock, equipment and goods in transitLoss or damage to your wax, oils, stock and kit, at home or on the way to a fairOptional; worth it once your stock is worth a lot

Public and products liability are often sold together in one policy. Check the policy wording to see that both are included, rather than assuming.

Craft fairs, markets & shops

Business.gov.uk notes that you need public liability if "venues or clients you work with require it in a contract", and product liability if "your retailers or stockists require you to have it". In practice, many craft fair organisers ask stallholders for proof of public liability cover, often with a minimum amount, and shops buying wholesale may ask for products liability.

Ask each organiser or stockist what they need before you book or sign, and leave yourself time to arrange cover. Keep a copy of your insurance certificate on your phone so you can show it on the day. Working from home: check your home insurance

Most wax melt businesses start at the kitchen table. Business.gov.uk advises updating your existing policies if you use your home or car for business. Melting wax, storing fragrance oils and keeping stock at home may not be covered by a standard home policy, so tell your home insurer what you do and ask. Not telling them could affect a claim.

If you employ only a close family member, GOV.UK says you don't need employers' liability insurance. Anyone else who works for you as an employee means you must have it, from an authorised insurer, for at least £5 million.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Does the policy cover both public and products liability, and does it name candles or wax melts as an activity?
  • What is the cover limit, and is it enough for the fairs or shops you sell to?
  • Does it cover online sales, and sales outside the UK if you post abroad?
  • What does it exclude? Business.gov.uk warns that "if something is not mentioned, it will not be covered."
  • Are there conditions, such as keeping safety data sheets, CLP labels or batch records? Our free batch log and CLP label checklist help you keep those in order.

Compare the cover, not just the price. You can buy direct from an insurer, through a broker, or via comparison sites, and some trade body memberships include insurance discounts.

Common questions

Is insurance a legal requirement to sell wax melts in the UK?

No, not if you work alone. GOV.UK and insurers agree that the only business insurance you must have by law is employers' liability (once you employ someone) and motor insurance if you use a vehicle for the business. Public and product liability are optional, but strongly recommended.

What insurance do wax melt makers usually get?

Public and products liability. Public liability covers injury or damage to members of the public, for example at a craft fair stall. Products liability covers claims that something you made or sold caused injury, illness or damage. Insurers often sell the two together in one policy.

Do craft fairs need me to have insurance?

Many do. Organisers can make public liability cover a condition of booking a stall, often with a minimum amount. Ask each organiser what they need before you book, and give yourself time to arrange it.

Does my home insurance cover my wax melt business?

Don't assume it does. GOV.UK advises updating your existing policies if you use your home or car for business. Tell your home insurer you make and store wax and fragrance oils at home, and ask what is and isn't covered.
