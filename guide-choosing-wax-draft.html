Types of Wax for Wax Melts: Which Is Best?

Soy, coconut blends, paraffin, rapeseed and beeswax compared for UK wax melt makers, with melt points and fragrance limits from the suppliers' own pages.

Home / Buying Guides / Wax Types

Short answer: The main types of wax for wax melts are soy, coconut blends, paraffin and rapeseed. For a UK home business, a soy wax made for melts, or a rapeseed and coconut melt blend, is the usual starting point. Both are plant-based, hold their shape in clamshells and moulds, and take up to about 10% fragrance oil, going by suppliers such as Candle Shack. Paraffin is made from petroleum and is often used for strong scent. Coconut wax is mostly sold blended, because the coconut blends made for candles melt low and stay soft. Avoid soft container candle wax for melts: it is made for jars, not for moulds.

A wax melt has to do things a candle never does. It has to hold its shape in a clamshell or mould, come out cleanly, carry fragrance, and melt fully in a warmer. That's why the wax matters more than almost anything else in your recipe. This guide compares the main types of wax for wax melts, using each supplier's published melt point and fragrance limit rather than opinions.

Caroline, who runs The Wax Biz, uses a rapeseed and coconut blend for her own melts. The figures below come from supplier pages, checked on 1 November 2026. Always check the product page or data sheet of the exact wax you buy, because blends change.

Candle wax vs wax melt wax

Most "types of wax" guides are written for candles. Container candle wax is made to be soft, so it sticks to a glass jar and melts in a wide pool around a wick. Melt waxes are made firmer so they release from moulds and keep their shape on a shelf.

You can see the difference in the suppliers' own specs. Candle Shack lists its Nature Wax C-3 container soy as not for wax melts. Its EcoSystem RCX container wax (rapeseed and coconut) has a melting point of around 40°C, while its RCX Melt Blend, made from the same two oils, melts at 47 to 53°C. Same plants, different job.

If your melts are sweating, sticking in the clamshell or going soft on the shelf, check whether your wax is a container wax before you change anything else. The troubleshooting guide covers the other common causes.

Soy wax and soy melt blends

Soy wax is made from soybean oil and is the most widely sold plant wax for melts in the UK. For melts, look for a soy wax sold for melts, not a container soy.

  • Example: Golden Wax 494 (GW494). Candle Shack gives its melting point as 52 to 58°C, a maximum of 10% fragrance oil, and a pouring temperature of 65 to 75°C. It describes GW494 as having excellent mould release.
  • Another: EcoSoya Melt, sold by several UK suppliers including Craftovator, Livemoor and FizzyWhiz.
  • Look: soy can develop frosting, a white crystal bloom on the surface. It changes how a melt looks, so test how your wax ages on a shelf.

Coconut wax and coconut blends

Coconut wax is rarely sold on its own for melts. It usually comes blended with soy, rapeseed or apricot wax. The coconut blends made for candles melt low: Candle Shack lists its EcoSystem SCX (soy and coconut) at 33 to 51°C and its RCX container wax at about 40°C. That's too soft for most moulds.

If you want coconut in your melts, choose a blend made for melts. Candle Shack's RCX Melt Blend is made only from rapeseed and coconut oils, melts at 47 to 53°C, and takes a maximum of 10% fragrance oil. Candle Shack says it comes out of moulds well and doesn't dip or form cavities.

Paraffin wax

Paraffin is made from petroleum. It's still common in wax melts, often blended with soy or vegetable waxes, and some makers choose it for strong scent and crisp detail in moulds. Some customers prefer plant waxes, so think about who you're selling to.

  • Example: Kerax Kerawax 4600, a paraffin blend pillar wax. Craftovator lists its melting point as 57°C and its fragrance load as up to 12%.
  • A soy and paraffin melt blend: Candle Shack's HPM Blend is made from soy and mineral waxes, melts at about 53°C and holds up to 10% fragrance. Candle Shack describes its scent throw as powerful.

A higher fragrance limit on the wax doesn't mean you can use more. The legal maximum for each fragrance oil in a wax melt is set by its IFRA certificate, category 12, which your fragrance supplier provides.

Rapeseed wax

Rapeseed wax is made from rapeseed (canola) oil. In UK melt waxes it usually appears in a blend, most often rapeseed and coconut, as in Candle Shack's RCX Melt Blend above. Rapeseed also appears in container waxes, such as Kerax's rapeseed and coconut container wax, which isn't made for melts. As with soy, check that the product says it's for melts.

Beeswax

Beeswax is rarely used for wax melts. It melts hotter than the melt waxes above: Craftovator lists yellow beeswax at 62 to 69°C. If you try it, test that your warmer melts it fully. Beeswax isn't vegan, so say so if you sell melts as vegan.

Which wax should you start with?

  • Your first batches: a wax sold for melts, such as a soy melt wax or a rapeseed and coconut melt blend. Both are plant-based and made to release from moulds.
  • Strong scent and crisp detail: some makers use a paraffin blend or a soy and paraffin melt blend. Test a few batches side by side before you decide.
  • Plant-based or vegan: soy or rapeseed and coconut melt blends. Check the supplier says the exact product is vegan-friendly.

There's no single best wax for every maker. The right one depends on your moulds, your warmer, your fragrances and your customers.

Test before you switch

Changing wax changes everything downstream: pour temperature, how fragrance binds, how melts release and how they look after a few weeks. Before you switch a whole range:

  1. Make a small test batch of each wax with the same fragrance and load. The fragrance load calculator keeps the percentage the same.
  2. Pour at each wax's own recommended temperature, from its product page.
  3. Cure, then test in your own warmer, and leave a few on a shelf for a couple of weeks to check for sweating or frosting.
  4. Record every test in the wax melt batch log so you can repeat the winner.

To work out how many melts a bag will make, use the melts per kg calculator. For how fragrance load affects throw, read the fragrance load guide.

Does wax type change my CLP label?

Your CLP label is based on your fragrance oils and colourants, using the Safety Data Sheets from their suppliers, and on your fragrance load. If you change wax but keep the same oils at the same load, check your label information again anyway, and keep your batch records. Use the CLP label checklist before you sell.

Questions

What types of wax can you use for wax melts?

Soy, coconut blends, paraffin, rapeseed and, rarely, beeswax. For melts, choose a wax the supplier says is made for melts, because container candle wax is too soft for moulds.

What is the best wax for wax melts in the UK?

There's no single best wax. A soy melt wax such as Golden Wax 494, or a rapeseed and coconut melt blend, is the usual starting point for a UK home business. Paraffin blends are often used for strong scent.

Can I use candle wax for wax melts?

Not container candle wax. It's made soft to stick to jars, so it can stick and sweat in clamshells. Candle Shack, for example, lists its Nature Wax C-3 container soy as not for wax melts.

Is coconut wax good for wax melts?

Coconut is used in blends. The coconut blends made for candles melt low (about 33 to 51°C), so choose a blend made for melts, such as a rapeseed and coconut melt blend at 47 to 53°C.

Soy or paraffin for wax melts?

Soy is plant-based and widely used. Paraffin is made from petroleum and is often chosen for strong scent and detail. Some suppliers sell soy and paraffin melt blends. Test both with your own fragrances before you decide.

What melt point do I need for a wax warmer?

The melt waxes above melt at about 47 to 58°C, according to their suppliers. Use the figure on your wax's product page or data sheet, and test in your own warmer.

How much fragrance can each wax take?

The melt waxes above list a maximum of 10% fragrance oil, and the paraffin blend 12%. The legal maximum for each oil is set by its IFRA certificate, category 12. Use the fragrance load calculator to get the amounts right.

Does wax type change my CLP label?

Your CLP label is based on your fragrance oils and colourants and your fragrance load, using their suppliers' Safety Data Sheets. Check the CLP label checklist whenever you change your recipe.
