Buying Guides & Tips

🕯️

Choosing the Right Wax

Soy, paraffin, coconut, beeswax or a blend? Each wax type offers different melt pools, scent throw and finish. Soy wax is popular for clean burning, while coconut blends give a creamy, glossy aesthetic ideal for clamshells.

Read Guide →
🌸

Fragrance Oil Load & Throw

Understanding fragrance load (typically 6–10% for wax melts) is key to a strong scent throw. Always test your chosen fragrance oil with your wax at different percentages before bulk buying.

Read Guide →
🎨

Colouring Your Wax Melts

Liquid dyes, dye chips and mica powders each create different effects. Liquid dyes give vivid, consistent colour; micas create shimmer but can affect skin-safe ratings — always check your supplier's specifications.

Read Guide →
🧪

Testing & Safety Standards

UK wax melt makers must comply with CLP (Classification, Labelling and Packaging) regulations. Ensure your fragrance oils come with Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and that you label products correctly before selling.

Read Guide →
📦

Packaging & Clamshells

Clamshells, snap bars, heart melts, shaped melts — your packaging choices affect both presentation and shelf life. Choose food-safe moulds for shapes and ensure packaging is clearly labelled for retail sale.

Read Guide →
💰

Buying Wholesale vs Retail

Many UK suppliers offer tiered pricing — buying 1kg vs 5kg of wax can significantly reduce your cost per unit. Several retailers on this directory offer dedicated wholesale accounts for small businesses.

Read Guide →
01
Always Test First

Before committing to a new fragrance or wax combination, always run a small test batch and cure for at least 48 hours.

02
Track Your Recipes

Keep a detailed log of every batch — wax type, fragrance percentage, pouring temp and colour. Consistency is key to building a reliable product range.

03
Check Flash Points

Your fragrance oil's flash point must be above your pouring temperature. This affects both safety and scent retention in the finished melt.

04
Join Maker Communities

UK Facebook groups and Reddit communities like r/candlemaking are invaluable for sharing supplier recommendations and troubleshooting issues.
